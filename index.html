Food & health insurance

SNAP (Food Stamps)

NJ is generous: 185% FPL, no asset limit, $95 minimum benefit, medical deductions available.

  • Expedited SNAP in about 7 days if you have under $100 cash & little income (up to 30 days if elderly/disabled)
  • Report income or household changes within 10 days; recertify on time so benefits don’t stop
  • Work rule (ABAWD) — since the 2025 federal changes, adults 18–64 with no child under 14 in the home are limited to 3 months in 3 years unless exempt (e.g. disabled) or working/training 80 hrs/mo

⚠ Not fully confirmed: the new work-rule ages (18–64, no child under 14) come from the 2025 federal law. Sources give different NJ start dates (Dec 2025 to Mar 2026), and some NJ counties may still have waivers. Ask the county or LSNJ whether the rule applies to you right now.

The marriage question

Be very careful before marrying for benefits. Marriage can raise household income limits for SNAP, Medicaid, LIHEAP, USF — but if either of you is on SSI, marriage can reduce or eliminate that benefit because spousal income counts. Married couples where both are disabled often do better than two individuals, but it depends on your specific income mix. Before deciding: call a benefits counselor through 211 or use the DCAid screening tool to model "what if I got married" scenarios.
How marriage can cost you Watch out
  • Two people on SSI get $994 + $994 = $1,988/mo single — but only $1,491/mo as a married couple (about $497/mo less, 2026)
  • If only one of you is on SSI, part of the other spouse’s income is counted against her check
  • Couple asset limit is $3,000, not $2,000 each
  • “Holding out” rule: if you live together and present yourselves as married (“my wife,” same last name, joint accounts as spouses), SSA can treat you as married for SSI even without a license. Be honest — just know the rule
  • Can trigger a tax marriage penalty on EITC if both of you earn

⚠ Not fully confirmed: the dollar figures are the 2026 federal SSI rates and don’t include NJ’s small state supplement. SSA decides the “holding out” rule case by case, based on how you present yourselves. Whether there’s an EITC marriage penalty depends on both of your incomes; VITA can run it both ways.

Hidden benefit: her parent’s Social Security Often missed
  • Autism starts in childhood. Adults disabled before age 22 can get Childhood Disability Benefits (DAC) on a parent’s record once that parent retires, becomes disabled, or dies
  • Pays 50% of the parent’s benefit (75% after death) — often more than SSI, and brings Medicare; Medicaid can be protected too
  • Marriage usually ends DAC — unless she marries someone who is also getting Social Security benefits (e.g. SSDI)
  • Same rule for your child later in life — keep every diagnosis, school & IEP record that proves disability began before 22

⚠ Not fully confirmed: a federal bill (H.R. 1389, Marriage Equality for Disabled Adults Act) would end the marriage rule for DAC, but it had not passed as of 2026. DAC also requires her parent to have enough Social Security work credits. Only SSA can say whether she qualifies, so ask them to check each parent’s record.

Apply smarter Biggest wins
  • Apply today, finish paperwork later — most benefits start from the date you file, not the date you’re approved. Submit a name, address & signature now; send proofs after
  • Medicaid pays old bills — NJ FamilyCare can cover up to 3 months before you applied. Starting Jan 1, 2027 that shrinks to 1–2 months, so apply before Dec 31 if you have recent medical bills
